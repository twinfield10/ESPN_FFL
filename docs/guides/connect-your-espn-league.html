Connect your ESPN league

Six things out of your ESPN account and I can pull your league into the projections. It takes about five minutes, once, and there is a form at the end to fill in and send straight back.

Before you start

You need a computer — laptop or desktop — and the Chrome browser. A phone cannot do this; the tool we need does not exist in the mobile app or in mobile Safari. Microsoft Edge works identically if that is what you have.

Log in at espn.com first and open the league you want connected, so you are looking at your own team.

1

Open your league and look at the address bar

With your league open, the web address at the top has a long number in it. That number is the league's ID. You do not have to pick it out — select the whole address and copy it, and I will take the number out of it.

2

Right-click anywhere on the page, then click Inspect

Right-click on an empty part of the page — not on a link or an image — and a menu appears. Inspect is at the very bottom. A second panel opens beside or underneath the page; that is normal, and nothing you do in there changes your team.

If right-clicking does nothing, press Option + Command + I on a Mac, or F12 on Windows.

3

In that new panel, click Application

The new panel has a row of words along its top: Elements, Console, Sources and so on. Click Application. If you cannot see it, the row has run out of space — click the » at the end of the row and pick Application from the list that drops down.

4

On the left, open Storage → Cookies → https://fantasy.espn.com

Down the left-hand side is a list. Find the Storage heading, click the little arrow next to Cookies to open it, and then click https://fantasy.espn.com. A big table fills the right-hand side.

5

Find the row called SWID and copy its Value

The table is sorted alphabetically and it is long — scroll until you find SWID in the left-hand Name column. Copy what sits next to it in the Value column.

Copy the curly braces at each end as well. They look like punctuation but they are part of the value, and it will not work without them.

6

Find the row called espn_s2 and copy its Value

Same table, a different row: espn_s2. This one is much longer and has % signs scattered through it. Because it is too long to fit the column, click the row once and the whole thing appears in a panel underneath, where you can select it and copy it.

If you see a checkbox marked “Show URL-decoded”, leave it unchecked. Ticking it quietly rewrites the value and the connection will fail.

7

Last thing: your name as ESPN spells it

My end matches you to your team by manager name, not by team name, and it has to match character for character — “Mike” and “Michael” are two different people to it. Open your league's Members page and tell me the name shown against your team, plus roughly what year the league started on ESPN.

Easiest of all: take a screenshot of that Members page and send it along. That answers the league name, your manager name and the spelling in one go.

If something looks different

  • No https://fantasy.espn.com in the list. Open your league page first, reload it, then look again. Failing that, click https://www.espn.com instead — the same two rows are in there.
  • The value looks chopped off. It is — the column is too narrow. Click the row and copy from the pane underneath.
  • It worked and then stopped a few weeks later. Expected. ESPN expires these on a schedule and I will ask you for a fresh pair; it is the same five minutes.

What you are actually sending me

Those two codes are the pass your browser shows ESPN to prove you are logged in. They are not your password, and they cannot be turned back into it — but while they last they do let me read ESPN as you, so treat them like a password: send them to me directly, not into a group chat, and do not post them anywhere public.

They expire on their own after a few weeks. If you ever want to cut them off sooner, change your ESPN password and both codes stop working immediately.
