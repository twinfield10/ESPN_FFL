## Why this is not just a fifth opinion

Every current source is a **projection** — somebody else's model output. Plan 03
already measured how correlated they are: averaged over 45 stats on Knights_FFL
2025, real (non-imputed) coverage was ESPN 100%, FantasyPros 13%, BetOnline 12%,
Pinnacle 8%. For most players `PINNY_*` *is* the ESPN/FantasyPros mean wearing a
sportsbook badge. Adding a sixth opinion of the same kind buys very little.

Usage data is a different kind of input: **observed process data**. Not "we think
he'll get 62 yards" but "he ran 41% of the routes, drew a 24% target share, and
earned 107 yards of opportunity while returning 55". None of the four sources
expose any of that, and it is the leading indicator of the thing they are all
trying to guess.

The bet is that a model built on usage produces errors **less correlated** with
the other four than they are with each other. That is what makes a blend better
rather than merely heavier — and it is measurable up front, so [§Step 0](#step-0--the-gates)
makes it the gate rather than an afterthought.

### Three claims from the earlier plans that are now wrong

1. ~~*"Snap counts are keyed on `pfr_player_id`, so they need their own crosswalk
   hop. Tier 2."*~~ `load_rosters_weekly` carries `gsis_id` **and** `pfr_id` in
   one frame, and `Data/NFL/player_ids.parquet` already keeps `pfr_id`
   (`R/GetPlayerIDs.R:51`). Snap share is a v1 feature.
2. ~~*"Route participation needs per-play list unnesting, so v2."*~~ Measured, it
   works: `load_participation(2025)` joins **100%** to `load_pbp(2025)` on
   `(game_id, play_id)` — 45,184 of 45,184 rows — giving 19,828 regular-season
   dropbacks, and unnesting `offense_players` yields **6,545 player-weeks with a
   routes count** for 2025 alone. `offense_players` coverage is 100% for 2023–25
   and 91.3–91.6% for 2016–22, so all ten seasons are usable.
3. ~~*"Raw `load_pbp()` is probably not needed for v1 at all."*~~ It is — as the
   join partner identifying dropbacks, and as the source of team pass-rate-over-
   expected and red-zone usage.

### Four traps, all found the hard way

- **`load_ff_opportunity` returns `season` and `week` as character.** The join to
  `calculate_stats` fails loudly on that, which is the good outcome, but cast on
  read.
- **`load_injuries`' `season_type` column is populated only for 2025.** Filtering
  `season_type == "REG"` silently drops 2016–2024 and leaves a plausible-looking
  table built from one season. It produced one wrong result while this plan was
  being written. Filter on `week <= 18`.
- **`calculate_stats`' `team` can disagree with participation's `posteam`** —
  Xavier Gipson, 2025 week 1: the stats frame says NYJ, participation says NYG,
  and joining route counts on the stats frame's team produced a route share of
  1.73. Route-share denominators must come from participation's own `posteam`.
- **`load_depth_charts` changed schema** between 2016 (15 cols) and 2025 (12).
  Normalise on read or restrict the depth-chart features to the newer window.

### The crosswalk, already done

`Scripts/crosswalk.py` + `R/GetPlayerIDs.R` ship the ID join this repo never had:
12,470 players, 7,908 `gsis_id`↔`espn_id` pairs, **98.5–99.0% coverage of
individual players across all nine boards**, every miss a D/ST team unit. Boards
carry `gsis_id`, so they are the bridge between ESPN's world and nflverse's.
Ambiguous upstream ids are refused rather than joined on.

`fantasypros_id` and `sleeper_id` in the same table are how [plan 20](20-consensus-sources.md)
joins new sources without touching the ~140 hand-curated rename entries.

## Measurements that decide the design

`Rscript R/UsageEvidence.R` regenerates all of this. 2016–2025.

### Opportunity is sticky; efficiency is not

Year over year, n = 2,252 player-season pairs (QB/RB/WR/TE, ≥8 games both seasons):

| Metric | r with next season |
|---|---|
| carries/game | **+0.915** |
| air-yards share | **+0.903** |
| WOPR | **+0.885** |
| target share | **+0.858** |
| expected points/game | +0.816 |
| actual points/game | +0.792 |
| yards per target | +0.577 |
| **TD rate** | **+0.234** |
| **points over expected/game** | **+0.193** |

This is the volume × efficiency split measured rather than asserted, and it sets
how hard to shrink each half. Touchdown rate and points-over-expected are close
to noise; taking either at face value is the classic way these models embarrass
themselves.

### Expected production beats actual production, weekly

Next-week PPR, RB/WR/TE, n = 42,796 player-weeks, in-sample OLS:

| Model | r | R² | resid sd |
|---|---|---|---|
| trailing-3 **actual** fantasy points | +0.520 | 0.2702 | 6.56 |
| trailing-3 **expected** fantasy points | +0.539 | 0.2907 | 6.46 |
| trailing-6 expected | +0.552 | — | — |
| both trailing-3 terms | — | 0.2978 | 6.43 |
| + trailing-6 expected | — | **0.3124** | 6.36 |
| + usage shares and over-expectation | — | 0.3153 | 6.35 |
| trailing-3 over-expectation alone | +0.090 | — | — |

Fitted jointly, the model puts **0.537 on expected and 0.234 on actual** — it
leans on what a player *earned* more than twice as hard as on what he *scored*.

By position (`t3_actual` → `t3_exp` → both → `+t6_exp` → `+usage`):

| Pos | n | actual | expected | both | +t6 | +usage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QB | 5,492 | .2084 | .2224 | .2334 | .2424 | .2425 |
| RB | 12,703 | .3035 | .3189 | .3269 | .3374 | .3389 |
| WR | 19,996 | .2441 | .2622 | .2707 | .2877 | **.2980** |
| TE | 10,097 | .2095 | .2399 | .2429 | .2586 | **.2700** |

**Three consequences, and they shape plans 18 and 19 more than any design
preference would have:**

- **The weekly head is where the payoff is, not the draft board.** At season
  level, expected PPG does *not* beat actual PPG at predicting next-season PPG
  (0.779 vs 0.792). The earlier plan 17 asserted the reverse. Its real pre-season
  value is that it is **more stable** (0.816 vs 0.792), which helps in a blend —
  not that it is a better point estimate.
- **Usage shares add almost nothing on top of expected production.** The jump
  from 0.2978 to 0.3124 is a longer expected-production window; the shares
  themselves are worth 0.0029 pooled. That kills a large amount of speculative
  feature engineering before it is written.
- **Except for WR and TE, where they are worth having** — +0.0103 and +0.0114,
  against +0.0001 for QB and +0.0015 for RB. So carry the shares, but scope the
  effort to the positions that pay for it.

### Injury data is genuinely predictive, and free

`load_injuries` over a 65,640 player-week grid (one row per week the player's
team played, after a 3-game baseline exists; 9.6% carry a report).
*% of baseline* is mean actual points as a share of mean baseline expected points.

| Week-N designation | n | % missed the game | % of baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Out | 2,379 | **100.0** | 0 |
| Doubtful | 393 | **99.2** | 1 |
| Questionable | 3,510 | 35.0 | 62 |
| not on report | 59,356 | 23.0 | 82 |

Out and Doubtful are nearly deterministic. Questionable is a coin-flip — which
that week's practice column resolves:

| Questionable, by practice participation | n | % missed | % of baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Did not participate | 542 | **57.2** | 38 |
| Limited | 2,272 | 33.5 | 63 |
| Full | 655 | 22.3 | 79 |

**No current source exposes this as a number.** ESPN puts an `injury_status`
string on the board; none of the four projection sources model availability at
all. A 57% vs 22% spread inside a single designation is a real edge, and it is
free.

Caveat to carry into plan 19: the 23% miss rate for players *not* on the report
is inflated, because the weekly report drops a player once he lands on IR and
this grid runs to season end for anyone who was ever active. Separating "hurt"
from "not on the roster" needs `load_rosters_weekly.status`.

### Coaching context: PFR is unavailable, Wikipedia is not

Pro-Football-Reference sits behind a Cloudflare managed challenge. Checked live:
even `/robots.txt` returns the JS-challenge interstitial, and
`/teams/nwe/2025.htm` is a **403** to a normal request. That is the same class of
control `STATE_OF_THE_REPO.md` declines to circumvent for BetOnline, and the
answer is the same here.

Wikipedia's MediaWiki API serves the same data and is meant to be used this way.
Every `<year>_<Team>_season` article carries `coach`, `off_coach`, `def_coach` in
its infobox — verified live, 2025 Texans → DeMeco Ryans / Nick Caley / Matt
Burke, HTTP 200. 32 teams × 2016–2026 is ~350 polite requests, cached once.

**State the limitation plainly: OC ≠ play-caller,** and no free source resolves
that. The scheme proxies below measure what the play-caller actually *does*,
which is the thing that moves projections; the Wikipedia table supplies the
*change* flag.

## Design

### It emits stat lines, not points

This falls out of the existing architecture rather than being a preference. The
repo's central idea is that projections are produced as stat lines and *then*
scored through each league's own rules — that is what lets one pipeline serve a
6-team standard league, a 16-team IDP league and a superflex without
special-casing.

So the models emit **`USG_<stat>`** columns — `USG_rushingYards`,
`USG_receivingReceptions`, `USG_passingTouchdowns` — that slot in beside
`ESPN_`/`FP_`/`PINNY_`/`BOL_` and get priced by `proj_to_score`. A model
predicting fantasy points directly could not serve nine leagues and would need
refitting per scoring system.

**The prefix is `USG_`, not the `PBP_` the earlier plan used.** Nothing is built
yet so this costs nothing, and `PBP_` would misdescribe a model fed by expected
production, team context and availability.

### Volume × efficiency, not yards in one step

```
opportunity                efficiency                      stat line
───────────                ──────────                      ─────────
expected production,   ×   yards per opportunity,     →     USG_receivingYards
routes, targets,           catch rate, TD rate              USG_receivingReceptions
carries, snap share        (shrunk toward positional        USG_rushingTouchdowns
(from trailing usage)       and air-yards baselines)
```

Two reasons, and the stickiness table above is the evidence for the first:

1. **The halves have very different signal.** Opportunity sits at r ≈ 0.86–0.92
   year over year; TD rate at 0.234 and points-over-expected at 0.193. Modelling
   them together lets the noisy half contaminate the predictable half.
2. **It is auditable.** When a projection is wrong you can see whether the model
   misjudged the opportunity or the efficiency. A single black-box yardage number
   tells you nothing, and this pipeline already has enough places where a wrong
   number looks plausible.

### Extraction layer

R, matching the `GetNFL.R` / `GetPlayerIDs.R` conventions: repo-root path
resolution, `stopifnot` validation *before* writing, season-scoped output, and a
pre-season no-data path that exits 0 rather than stack-tracing.

| Script | Writes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `R/GetUsage.R` | `Data/NFL/<season>/{player_weeks,opportunity,routes}.parquet` | `calculate_stats(s,"week","player")`, `load_ff_opportunity`, and the participation×pbp route derivation |
| `R/GetContext.R` | `Data/NFL/<season>/{injuries,snap_counts,depth_charts,rosters_weekly}.parquet` | availability and role |
| `Scripts/coaches.py` | `Data/NFL/coaching_staff.parquet` | Wikipedia MediaWiki API; Python because it is not an nflverse pull |

Backfill 2016–2025 once (~3 min), append weekly thereafter. Kept out of
`GetNFL.R` so the weekly schedule refresh stays fast.

`coaching_staff.parquet` is **committed** — small, hand-auditable, and the
upstream can change, the same argument that keeps `player_ids.parquet` in git.
The season-scoped nflverse pulls are **gitignored**: regenerable and ~70 MB.

### Feature layer

`Scripts/usage/features.py`, Polars — per `CLAUDE.md` and
`STATE_OF_THE_REPO.md`'s "new code should be Polars". Four families:

| Family | Features | From |
|---|---|---|
| **Opportunity** | trailing 3/6/season/prior-season expected production; target and air-yards share; WOPR; route share; snap share; carries; red-zone and goal-line touches | ff_opportunity, calculate_stats, participation, snap_counts, pbp |
| **Efficiency** | `*_diff` (actual − expected); yards per route run; RACR/PACR; YAC over expected; TD rate vs expected TD — all shrunk toward positional baselines | ff_opportunity, NGS |
| **Context** | team pass rate over expected, pace, implied team total, opponent defensive strength, roof/surface/rest, personnel and formation rates, play-action/motion/no-huddle, head-coach and OC change flags | pbp, schedules, participation, FTN, coaching_staff |
| **Availability** | week-N report status × practice status, games missed in the trailing window, depth-chart rank, roster status, **teammate absence** | injuries, rosters_weekly, depth_charts |

**Teammate absence is called out deliberately.** A WR2's target share when the
WR1 is Out is where injury data earns most of its value, and it is computable
from injuries + depth charts and from nothing else here.

Weight the effort by §Measurements: trailing expected production first, usage
shares second and mainly for WR/TE, over-expectation shrunk to near zero.

### Leakage discipline

Every feature for week *N* is built from weeks strictly before *N*. This is the
one thing that will silently make the backtest look excellent and the live model
useless. Feature construction takes the target week as an argument and filters on
it.

The week-*N* injury report is **not** leakage: it is published Wednesday to
Friday, before Sunday's games. `date_modified` is the audit column if that ever
needs proving.

Two tests, both in `tests/test_usage_features.py`:

- A feature frame for week *N* is unchanged when week *N*'s stat rows are altered.
- A feature frame built at week *N* equals the same slice of a frame built at
  week *N+5*.

### It cannot help week 1, and should say so

There is no 2026 play-by-play, because no 2026 games have been played.

- **Draft board** — prior-season usage is genuinely useful and is the signal
  here. See [plan 18](18-season-usage-model.md).
- **Weekly** — no current-season data until games exist. It **abstains** rather
  than emitting noise, coming online around week 3–4. See
  [plan 19](19-weekly-usage-model.md).

Abstaining already works: plan 07 made a wholly-absent source degrade correctly.
`impute_columns` creates the columns from `MEAN_`, flags every cell, and
`compute_weighted_stats` renormalises it out of `TRUE_*`. A `USG_` source
returning an empty frame behaves exactly like pre-season Pinnacle does today, and
the app's coverage panel shows it at 0%.

## Step 0 — the gates

**Built first, roughly a day, and it decides everything downstream.** Do not
build features before this returns a number.

1. **Build the evaluation set.**
   ```bash
   python -m Scripts.refresh --all --season 2025
   ```
   One row per player-week carrying the actual outcome (`points`), all four
   sources' stat lines, and the `*_is_imputed` provenance flags — so accuracy is
   measured on cells where a source *really* had a line, rather than scoring ESPN
   against its own imputed copy.

   Reachability confirmed 2026-08-06: ESPN still serves 2025 (Knights_FFL,
   `current_week` 17, week-1 box scores carrying `points` **and**
   `projected_points`), and all four sources' 2025 archives are on disk under
   `Data/Projections/*/{Season,Landing}/2025/`. FantasyPros URLs take no season
   parameter, so that archive cannot be re-scraped — it is the only copy.

2. **Fit the crudest usage baseline** — the two-term `t3_actual + t3_exp`
   regression from §Measurements. Nothing clever. The point is to have residuals.

3. **Publish the pairwise residual-correlation matrix** across
   ESPN / FP / PINNY / BOL / usage-baseline against 2025 actuals, on non-imputed
   cells only. Write it into this document.

   This one artifact answers two separate questions: the model's independence
   gate, *and* [plan 20](20-consensus-sources.md)'s consensus question — because
   the marginal value of source *k+1* is roughly (1 − its correlation with the
   rest).

### Go / no-go

Stated as measured comparisons, not magic thresholds:

- **G0 — independence.** Usage residuals materially less correlated with ESPN's
  than ESPN's are with FantasyPros'. If they are highly correlated with ESPN's,
  this is re-deriving what ESPN already knows and the whole exercise is a
  rounding error. **Stop, and record the number here** rather than quietly
  shelving it.
- **G1 — accuracy.** Adding `USG_` reduces blended per-stat MAE on the 2025
  holdout. If it does not, do not ship it at a token weight — say so here.
- **G2 — draft.** Within-position Spearman against realised season points
  improves over `TRUE_` alone. This is the board's actual objective and it is not
  the same test as G1.

## Risks

- **The largest part is not the model.** It is leakage-free feature construction
  and an honest backtest.
- **Correlated residuals** would make the whole thing marginal. Hence G0.
- **Positional coverage.** Rich for RB/WR/TE, thinner for QB (§Measurements: usage
  shares add 0.0001 there), absent for K and D/ST. The models emit nothing for
  positions they cannot serve — the absent-source path, not a positional default.
  A model that quietly emits a positional average has apparent full coverage and
  drags the blend toward the mean for exactly the players a board most needs to
  differentiate. D/ST is [plan 13](13-dst-from-vegas-lines.md)'s job.
- **A fifth source changes the renormalisation arithmetic** in
  `compute_weighted_stats`. It handles an arbitrary source set already, but the
  hand-tuned `WEIGHTS` sum to 1.0 across four names and will need re-deriving —
  [plan 03](03-projection-source-coverage.md)'s job, and why this feeds into it.
- **Upstream dependency.** `load_ff_opportunity` is `ffverse/ffopportunity`
  release data, not nflverse core. If it stops updating mid-season the weekly
  head loses its best feature. Record the release timestamp with each pull so a
  stale file is visible rather than silently reused.
