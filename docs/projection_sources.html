Which sources depend on which

The honest answer to “is this source independent?” is the imputation chain. When a source has no line for a player, its cell is filled from somewhere else and flagged. A book with no opinion on a player is therefore ESPN and FantasyPros wearing a sportsbook badge — and if that copy voted at face value it would count the same two sources three or four times over.

The Athletic feeds the mean as well as filling from it, and that is not circular. MEAN_ averages only the cells that are genuine opinions, and a source's gaps are filled after it is built — so where The Athletic speaks it contributes, and where it is silent the mean is exactly what it was before. It joined on 2026-09-02 for one reason above all: MEAN_ decides what a book "says" about the ~97% of players it does not price, and FantasyPros publishes no receivingTargets at all, so the basis for the thinnest-covered stat on the board had been ESPN averaged with a copy of itself.

TOMCAT has no arrow into it, and that is a decision rather than an oversight. Filling the one genuinely independent source from an average of two that are not would turn it into a copy of them — the exact double-counting the flags exist to prevent. Its gaps stay null, stay flagged, and simply do not vote.

Strengths

  • The most independent source in the blend by a distance — +0.113 residual independence against the best external's +0.068, and +0.371 partialled against +0.180. External consensus is a saturated channel; this is not part of it.

It cuts a listed WR1 about 4% and gives a listed rank 2 the 16.7% of the time he is actually the lead.
  • A veteran who missed a whole season is projected rather than skipped. The rate ladder falls back to a pooled positional baseline — QB 7.12 yards an attempt, RB 4.27 a carry — so a hundred carries cannot come out as five yards. The baseline arm names them.
  • Decomposes into volume × efficiency × games, so a disagreement can be read rather than only noted.

Weaknesses and bias

  • Under-spread on receiving volume — RB receptions 1.315, TE receiving yards 1.370, QB rushing TDs 1.510 against realised. It shrinks toward positional baselines where the others extrapolate. Correcting it alone would make the board worse: its error and FantasyPros' point in opposite directions and partly cancel.
  • Its availability arm is its weakest: prior-season games predict next season at r = +0.343.
  • Covers only QB/RB/WR/TE — silent on kickers and defences.
  • Excluded from the floor/ceiling spread on purpose: it answers "what does the model expect" rather than "what do forecasters disagree about", and mixing the two makes the interval read as bearish rather than uncertain.

Which way each source is biased

Every number is a ratio against the ESPN/FantasyPros mean on exactly the rows that source was real for. Above 1.000 means it projects more of that stat than ESPN and FantasyPros do about the same players; below means less. The p10–p90 spread column is the same comparison applied to the width of the distribution rather than its centre, so a source that agrees on the average player but is too timid at the top reads as a low number there and a 1.000 beside it.

The pairing is the method. A source's real cells are a biased sample of the pool — a book prices the players worth pricing — so comparing its 30 lines against a 500-row baseline would measure coverage and call it bias. The baseline is restricted to the source's own rows every time.

Gated at 100 projected points by ESPN or FantasyPros, and built from those two only so no source can be accused of selecting the rows that flatter it. Ungated, percentage disagreement divides by the projection, so two sources eight and thirteen points apart on a third-string quarterback read as 47% disagreement about nobody — and that noise swamps every row that matters.

Stat-level bias is league-independent: these are raw stat lines, and nothing here has been scored. Unlike every points number on this page, this section holds for all nine leagues.

Read the ESPN and FantasyPros rows as the reference. They bracket the baseline by construction — where both are real on the same rows their two mean ratios sum to exactly 2.000, and they differ only where one of them is null and the other is not. How far apart they sit is how far apart the two sources everyone already trusts are, on this stat, at this position. A third source sitting closer to 1.000 than that gap is not disagreeing enough to be worth adjusting for.

A ratio here is not always a level bias, and the rushing rows are the worked example. The Athletic reads 0.77 on rushingAttempts, which looks like a source that is bearish on running backs. It is not. Summed to team rushing budgets it sits at 0.970 of ESPN — the two agree almost exactly on how often a team runs. What they disagree about is who gets the ball: the lead back's share of his own backfield is 0.675 for ESPN and 0.645 for The Athletic, so on backs ESPN gives 150+ carries it reads 0.923 and on backs ESPN gives under 60 it reads 1.495. It flattens backfields.

The 100-point gate is what turns that into a level ratio, and it is worth knowing before adjusting anything. The gate keeps the top of every room and drops the bench, so a source that redistributes within a room shows up here as though it were low on the whole stat. Correcting that with a multiplier would be precisely wrong — it would restore the starters and leave the committee view, which is the actual opinion, uncorrected and now double-counted. Read a rushing row next to the team-budget check, not on its own.

Note what the two reference rows do not show: a level difference. Across gated receivers their mean receiving yards agree to within 0.1%, while individual players disagree by around 10%. ESPN and FantasyPros disagree about players, not about totals — so a source that is off at the aggregate is saying something neither of them is.

The weight that is nominal, and the weight that is real

Every universal source carries a nominal 0.25. Almost none of them ever gets it. Because the nominal figures are identical, renormalising over the sources that are real on a row makes the rule literal — one equal vote per source that has an opinion. Four real sources weight 0.25 each, three weight 0.333, two weight 0.5.

So this table, not the 0.25, is what to reason with when deciding whose bias is worth correcting. A source biased 20% low on a stat where it realises 0.05 of the weight is moving the board by one point in a hundred.

How a final projection is built

Sources are blended as stat lines and scored afterwards. That one decision is what lets a single pipeline serve nine leagues with different rules: there is one blended stat line per player, and nine different sets of rules priced against it. It also means the headline identity —

TRUE_Points = score(TRUE_ stat line), not mean(ESPN_Points, FP_Points, …)

— and the two are genuinely different numbers. Averaging the points columns on this page will not reproduce the blend, and it is not supposed to.

The weighting rule

TRUE_<stat> = Σ(ws · xs · reals) / Σ(ws · reals)

where reals is 0 when the cell was filled in and 1 when the source genuinely projected it. A source with no column at all is skipped from both sums — an earlier bug skipped it from the numerator only, which halved projections purely because Pinnacle was absent. Where every source for a stat is imputed the denominator is zero and it falls back to the face-value sum, which cannot happen in production because ESPN always carries weight and is never imputed.

The order it runs in

  1. Load every source into <SRC>_<stat> columns, joining on a disambiguated name key — or on player_id for TOMCAT, the only source with ids.
  2. _apply_injury_adjustment and _withdraw_usage_on_role — TOMCAT alone is scaled for expected games, because ESPN and FantasyPros already price a known absence.
  3. _withdraw_sources_on_availability — three gates. Out for the season; ESPN prices zero and he is out; ESPN prices zero and only one source is left. Two sources disagreeing with an ESPN zero are deliberately left alone.
  4. impute_columns — fill the holes, flag every filled cell.
  5. _make_usage_coherent — rescale TOMCAT to a full 17-game slate so all six sources describe the same quantity.
  6. compute_weighted_stats — the formula above.
  7. reconcile_team_totals — close the team identities.
  8. redistribute (vacancy transfer), then reconcile_team_totals again — a back who inherits receiving work was thrown to by somebody, and the identity is what carries the matching volume onto the quarterbacks who threw it.
  9. attach_milestone_bands — after the blend, because a milestone bonus is a step function of a weekly quantity rather than a rate on a season total.
  10. proj_to_score — every source's line and the blend, priced through each league's own rules, so they can be compared rather than only the blend published.
  11. VOR, tiers, vor_rank, dollar allocation; Jake's Ranks attached last as a display overlay that changes nothing.

Read the MEAN row against the imputed ones: (1,281.9 + 1,994.0) / 2 = 1,741.2, and every imputed cell is that number exactly. That is what a filled cell is — not a projection, but the two sources it was filled from, which is precisely why it must not vote.

The blend is not the last step. The weighted average above is 1,891.6; the board's TRUE_passingYards is 1,919.8, a move of +28.2. That gap is reconcile_team_totals, which scales a team's passing and receiving sides to their midpoint so the line describes a team that could actually have produced it. No single source is complete enough to hold that identity alone, which is why it happens after the blend rather than before.

Two tools, and they do not see the same sources

This is the largest structural asymmetry in the system and it is easy to miss, because both views render the same column names.

Three of the six votes that build a draft board are structurally absent week to week, and a fourth has no live data. The draft board is a six-source blend; the weekly view is an ESPN/FantasyPros blend with a thin sportsbook overlay. They are not the same instrument and should not be read as though a number carried the same weight of evidence in both.

The gap is named work rather than an oversight — docs/plans/19-weekly-usage-model.md is the weekly usage head, and it is where the larger edge is thought to be, precisely because the weekly path is the thinner of the two.

What would make this more accurate

Ranked by how much they would move a projection against how well understood they are. Each is tied to a measurement already in the repo rather than an instinct.

  1. The Athletic's Kenny Gainwell join miss
    The workbook spells him Kenneth, so he silently fell through the name join and his cells were filled from MEAN_ instead. Resolved on this board — his Athletic line is real. The alias has landed. One line in NAME_ALIASES fixes it. It is deliberately unapplied in draft week because it moves TRUE_Points, and that is a decision to take on purpose rather than as a side effect of a docs change.
  2. De-vig Pinnacle's season props
    The season path pivots on the raw line and never reads the no_vig_over_prob its own scraper already computes. BetOnline's season path uses a juice-adjusted True_Line, and Pinnacle's weekly path inverts the line properly — so the draft board is the one place in the system where a book's vig is left in. The arithmetic already exists; it is a wiring gap, not a modelling one.
  3. Measure The Athletic out of sample, on a season basis
    It carries a full equal vote and its per-stat MAE has never been measured. The existing harness cannot do it: lab.accuracy, usage.gates and g1_season all score player-week rows out of lineups.parquet, and this source is season-only with no column there. That is the trap — run as-is, they would produce a clean table in January that looked like the source had been judged when it had not. What is owed is a season-basis harness scoring board.parquet's ATH_<stat> against realised season totals.
  4. Fix rushingTouchdowns, the one stat the blend loses on
    Scored per stat, the shipping blend is worse than ESPN, FantasyPros and Pinnacle on rushing touchdowns, on all three populations, while the fantasy-point MAE for the same league-season reads a clean win. Points MAE cannot see it because yardage carries the variance — which is the general lesson: a points-level metric will hide a stat-level defect every time, and this page's bias tables exist to be read at the stat level for that reason.
  5. Do not correct TOMCAT's receiving spread on its own
    It is under-spread on receiving volume — RB receptions 1.315, TE receiving yards 1.370 against realised — and it is also the best-calibrated single source in the blend. FantasyPros runs 0.65–0.95 and BetOnline 0.21–0.81 in the opposite direction, so the errors partly cancel and fixing one arm in isolation makes the board worse. This is the strongest argument on the page for adjusting sources jointly, against the blended line, rather than one at a time.
  6. Build the weekly heads
    Half the sources vanish week to week. The weekly usage head is the named work and is where the larger edge is thought to be.
  7. Re-run the kicking gate now the column names are right
    The arm was held at 0.0 on G-K2, and the points that made it look unusable were a defect rather than a model: it emitted madeFieldGoalsFromFrom40To49 for two of three distance bands, which no league scores, so two thirds of a kicker's field-goal value was silently zero and the arm read at 0.577× ESPN. Corrected it reads 0.946× and it now votes. G-K2 was measured on stats, so the typo does not overturn it — but it was last run against a model whose own report tables were built from the miswritten columns, and re-running it is cheap. Until then a kicker's number is a deliberate override of a failed gate.
  8. Give Jake's Ranks somewhere to go
    The hand ranking is display-only because the blend works in stat space and an ordering has nowhere to sit in it. But its shape is informative: against the order his own projections imply, no quarterback moves five spots and 55 of 120 receivers do — he leaves alone the position a projection handles best and reworks the two it handles worst. That is the same inversion the 100-point gate finds in the stat lines, reached independently, and it is an argument for using it as a tier prior at receiver rather than a column.
